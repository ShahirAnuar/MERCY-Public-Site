The official book reference

MERCY

By Shahir Anuar Shahrani, also published as Shahir Anuar

MERCY by Shahir Anuar Shahrani is an illustrated Islamic story about Zayd and his father, Khalid. Through conversations about mistakes, sincere questions, learning differently and achievement, they explore repentance, gratitude and compassion. The story asks readers to value people beyond grades, perfection or the struggles others can see.

What is MERCY about?

Zayd finds ordinary tasks and school difficult. Mistakes leave him ashamed, and his need to understand things makes him worry that he asks too many questions. Khalid listens to his son and helps him approach those experiences through faith, patience and the possibility of beginning again.

As Zayd grows, he faces prejudice and the pressure to prove himself. His father's guidance also turns toward success: doing well does not make a person superior, and effort can lead to gratitude instead of pride. The relationship works in both directions, with father and son learning from each other.

The book brings together three connected parts: The Balance, The Boy Who Asked Too Much, and Mercy. Its central themes are Allah's mercy, sincere questions, repentance, hidden struggles, humility and kindness toward oneself and others.

Who might enjoy reading MERCY?

MERCY may suit families looking for an illustrated Islamic story that opens conversations about self-worth, learning differently and faith. Parents and educators can use Zayd and Khalid's conversations to discuss mistakes, school pressures and compassion with young readers.

The story includes bullying, illness, medication and physical limitations. Adults can preview those passages and choose a reading pace that suits the child. Its themes may resonate with neurodivergent readers and families, but it is a work of storytelling, not a diagnostic guide or a treatment programme.

Questions about MERCY

Is MERCY an Islamic children's book?

Yes. MERCY is an illustrated story for young readers and families, with Islamic themes including Allah's mercy, repentance, gratitude and humility. Zayd's relationship with his father gives those themes a personal setting.

Does MERCY explore learning differences and self-worth?

Yes. Zayd struggles to learn in the way others expect and feels judged for difficulties they cannot see. The story explores how a family can respond with understanding and how a child's value extends beyond performance.
