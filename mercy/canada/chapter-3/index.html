Start with the story

Achievement is only one part of a life

Zayd earns strong results after years of work, but other people’s rumours make him question his achievement. His father reminds him that grades do not measure his worth. Their conversation moves through pride, unfairness, health, treatment, disability, gratitude and what it means to succeed. By the end, Zayd has taught his father, too.

Big question: How can we work hard, celebrate progress and stay grateful—without using success or hardship to feel superior to others?
Health, faith and dignity

The story challenges the idea that prescribed medicine automatically means weak faith. It also recognizes side effects, stigma, tremor and grief when Zayd loses an artistic outlet. Discuss health gently and generally. No reader needs to share a diagnosis or treatment. This story guide is not medical advice; real treatment decisions belong with the person and a qualified health professional.

Success, support and fairness

Zayd’s effort matters. So do support from family, learning opportunities and the gifts he understands as coming from Allah. The chapter invites readers to notice advantages we may overlook and to question rumours and assumptions. Gratitude can honour hard work without pretending anyone succeeds alone.

Hardship, pride and compassion

Pain is real, but it does not make one person better than everyone else. Sharing a struggle can help another person feel less alone. It can also become a way of claiming status if we use it to rank people. The chapter encourages compassion for visible and invisible difficulties, including our own.

Ambition with mercy

Khalid asks Zayd not to study so someone else fails, worship so another person looks smaller, or wish for someone’s downfall. The invitation is to become better than we were yesterday. Effort and gratitude can grow together.
