A companion to MERCY

Read, pause, talk.

This family and classroom guide uses MERCY by Shahir Anuar Shahrani to start conversations about mistakes, questions, gratitude and compassion. Choose one question at a time and let readers respond in words, drawings or a quiet moment of thought.

Updated 13 October 2026 · New companion activities from the author's website

How can we read MERCY together?

Begin with a short section. Ask what the reader noticed before offering your own interpretation. Children can discuss Zayd's experience without sharing a personal story. Leave room for disagreement and for returning to a question later.

What can we discuss after Zayd makes a mistake?

  • What is Zayd afraid his mistakes say about him?
  • What does Khalid do that helps his son feel heard?
  • How could a character put one small thing right without expecting perfection?

Activity: Draw two pictures: a difficult moment, then one possible next step. The second picture does not need to show everything fixed.

How does the story make room for sincere questions?

  • Why might a child hesitate to say “I don't understand”?
  • What changes when an adult listens with patience?
  • Which question from the story would you like to explore together?

Activity: Make a shared question page. Put a question on it and return when you have time to discuss it or find a reliable answer. Not knowing immediately can be part of learning.

What is the difference between gratitude and feeling superior?

  • Who supports Zayd when school becomes difficult?
  • Why does Khalid talk about humility after Zayd succeeds?
  • How can we celebrate an achievement while recognising help and opportunity?

Activity: Draw a tree of support. Add a leaf for each person, opportunity or kindness that helped a character. Discuss how effort and gratitude can exist together.

How can readers talk about hidden struggles?

Use the fictional characters as a starting point. Ask what classmates might misunderstand and how they could behave more kindly. Avoid asking a child to disclose a diagnosis or explain private experiences to a group. Offer the choice to listen, draw, write or skip a question.

The book includes illness, medication, bullying and physical limitations. Keep discussion focused on dignity and the story. Questions about a real person's treatment belong with that person's healthcare professional.

Where should we start?

Read the MERCY summary and edition guide, then choose the format that suits your household or classroom. These activities are suggestions for discussion; they have not been evaluated as a therapeutic or educational intervention.
