The Boy Who Asked Too Much

What makes a question brave? Join Zayd as he asks, listens, learns and keeps searching.

← Previous pageNext page →

Start with the story

There is no shame in asking sincerely

Zayd wants to understand, not to mock belief. His father welcomes the question and tells him about Prophet Ibrahim’s search for truth. The chapter encourages thoughtful questions, patient learning and humility. It does not ask readers to accept every claim without understanding—or to dismiss everything before learning.

Two ways of knowing: ‘aqli and naqli

‘Aqli relates to reason and thinking. Naqli relates to knowledge transmitted through revelation and reliable reports. These terms can help us discuss different sources of knowledge. They do not mean one must replace the other. Ask: What does this claim mean? What evidence supports it? Is this a verse, a translation, an interpretation or someone’s opinion?

Think for yourself—and learn from others

The Qur’an challenges people who say they will only follow what they found their forefathers doing (Qur’an 2:170). It invites people to reflect on creation (Qur’an 3:190–191), and reminds us that our hearing, sight and intellect carry responsibility (Qur’an 17:36). These verses encourage us to use our minds sincerely, reflect and avoid claiming knowledge without learning. Questioning inherited ideas does not mean disrespecting family. It means learning sincerely, checking what you hear and asking trustworthy, knowledgeable people.

The question “Is there a God?” is one of the biggest questions people face. The question of what happens after death follows closely. Each person needs room to think, ask, read, learn and reflect. No child should be mocked for wondering or pressured to pretend certainty. Keep asking with honesty and care.

Does Allah promise to draw near to the seeker?

A hadith qudsi recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari 7405 says: “If he comes to Me walking, I go to him running.” It describes Allah’s nearness to a servant who draws near to Him. This is a promise of divine welcome and nearness, not a guarantee that every question will receive an instant answer or that Allah’s nearness should be imagined as physical movement. Zayd’s story reminds us that some answers take time.

Asking, learning and seeking truth sincerely can be part of turning toward Allah. Keep learning, and ask a trusted teacher when you want deeper context.
