What happens when a child keeps asking?

Zayd finds ordinary tasks and school difficult. He worries about mistakes and whether he asks too many questions. Khalid listens, helping his son consider faith, patience and the possibility of beginning again. As the story turns toward achievement, it also asks what success means when other people are still struggling.

The book brings together The Balance, The Boy Who Asked Too Much and Mercy. Its Qur'an-inspired themes include repentance, humility and Allah's mercy. Readers meet a father and son who learn from one another, and an invitation to value people beyond grades or perfection.

Make room for a family read-aloud

A Muslim family read-aloud can begin with one short passage. Pause where your reader notices something: a question, a difficult feeling or a choice a character makes. Ask what they saw before asking what it means. There is room to disagree, return to the page and think again.

Compassion

Ask, “What might we miss if we judge someone by what they find difficult?” Let the conversation stay close to the character's experience.

Gratitude

Notice a moment of achievement. Ask how someone could feel pleased with their effort while remaining humble and considerate of others.

Learning differently

Explore how listening changes a conversation. Readers can respond aloud, draw an idea or take time before answering. Choose an approach that suits your family.

Is MERCY an English-language Islamic children's book?

Yes. The MERCY Kindle, paperback and hardcover editions linked here are in English. The free reading guide offers explanatory answers in English and Bahasa Melayu; that does not mean the book editions are translated.

Can I use MERCY for homeschool or classroom discussion?

Families and educators can use the free reading guide to plan conversations and activities. Preview the story, language and themes to decide how to use it with your readers; no fixed reading-age claim is made here.
